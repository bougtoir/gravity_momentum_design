# Verified Reference Library — gravity_momentum_design

All entries were located via live searches (2026-09-26). DOIs/URLs recorded only where
verified from the source listing. Sources are grouped by role in the manuscript.
Citation keys used in manuscript source are in [brackets].

## A. Partial-gravity human biomechanics (established knowledge)

1. [Newman1992] Newman, D.J. *Human locomotion and energetics in simulated partial
   gravity*. PhD thesis / MIT report, MIT, 1992. http://hdl.handle.net/1721.1/13172
   — Parabolic flight + underwater partial-g locomotion; peak vertical force and
   stride frequency decrease with g; loping gait at lunar/Martian g.

2. [Richter2017] Richter, C. et al. "Human biomechanical and cardiopulmonary
   responses to partial gravity – a systematic review." *Frontiers in Physiology* 8
   (2017). PMC5559498. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5559498/
   — 43 studies; GRF, mechanical work, stance duration, stride frequency, duty
   factor, walk–run transition all reduced in 0.1–0.4 g.

3. [Macaulay2024] (npj Microgravity) "Functional activities essential for space
   exploration performed in partial gravity during parabolic flight." *npj
   Microgravity* (2024). https://www.nature.com/articles/s41526-024-00422-3
   — 12 subjects, 0.25/0.5/0.75/1.0 g parabolas; sit-to-stand, walking, jumping
   down, fall recovery, stance; task times and falls increased as g decreased.
   Key external-validation target (Level A/B).

4. [Foster2012 / TASJ2012] "Simulation studies of bipedal walking on the Moon and
   Mars." *Trans. JSASS Aerospace Technology Japan* 10 (2012), pp_5–.
   https://doi.org/10.2322/tastj.10.pp_5

5. [Sylos2021] "Human muscle activity and lower limb biomechanics of overground
   walking at varying levels of simulated reduced gravity and gait speeds."
   *J. Appl. Physiol.* / PMC8279339 (2021).
   https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8279339/
   — 1.0/0.76/0.55/0.31 g body-weight support; peak GRF and joint moments decrease,
   muscle activity non-linearly related to g.

## B. NASA standards, simulation facilities, crew-induced loads

6. [NASA-STD-3001V2] NASA Spaceflight Human System Standard, Volume 2: Human
   Factors, Habitability, and Environmental Health (NASA-STD-3001-V2).
   https://www.nasa.gov/reference/nasa-std-3001v2/ — architecture, habitability,
   crew interfaces, restraints/mobility-aid requirements for vehicles and
   lunar/Martian habitats.

7. [Bekdash2020] Bekdash, O. et al. "Development and evaluation of the Active
   Response Gravity Offload System as a lunar and Martian EVA simulation
   environment." NASA NTRS 20205003610 (2020).
   https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20205003610

8. [Valle2023] Valle, P.S. et al. "A preliminary assessment of physical demand
   during simulated lunar surface extravehicular activities." IEEE Aerospace
   Conference (2023). https://doi.org/10.1109/AERO55745.2023.10115603

9. [JSC2014Perf] "Human performance in simulated reduced gravity environments."
   NASA NTRS 20140017135 (2014). https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20140017135
   — ARGOS vs C-9 ambulation, lunar 1.6 m/s² vs Earth.

10. [EDLS2001] "Astronaut-induced disturbances to the microgravity environment of
    the Mir space station." *J. Spacecraft and Rockets* (2001).
    https://doi.org/10.2514/2.3719 — EDLS foot-restraint/handhold load sensors;
    2806 disturbances; max force 137 N; 96% below 60 N. Direct empirical basis
    for interface reaction-force magnitudes in weightlessness.

11. [CrewLoads1987] "Crew-induced load measurement for space operations." *Proc.
    Human Factors Society* 31 (1987), 725. https://doi.org/10.1177/154193128703100725

12. [MITMICROG2001] *Electronic architecture and technology development of
    astronaut spaceflight load sensors* (MICRO-G), MIT thesis (2001).
    http://hdl.handle.net/1721.1/83679

## C. Friction, stability, falls (terrestrial biomechanics baseline)

13. [RCOF-incline2022] "Quantification of gait stability during incline and decline
    walking: responses of required coefficient of friction and dynamic postural
    index." PMC9581656 (2022). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9581656/

14. [Matsumoto2024] "Effects of foot–ground friction and age-related gait changes
    on falls during walking: a computational study using a neuromusculoskeletal
    model." *Scientific Reports* (2024).
    https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-81361-7 — friction coefficient varied
    0.05–2.0; slip- vs trip-induced falls; optimal friction concept.

15. [STS-forces] "Coordinated ground forces exerted by buttocks and feet are
    adequately programmed for weight transfer during sit-to-stand." *J.
    Biomechanics* (1999). PubMed 10601437 — buttock/foot A/P forces, impulse
    structure of STS.

16. [STS-moments] "Computation of the kinematics and the minimum peak joint
    moments of sit-to-stand movements." *Med. Eng. Phys.* PMC1929086.
    https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1929086/ — COP must remain inside
    support base; inverse-dynamics STS library.

17. [StairCOP] "Relationship between stair ambulation with and without a handrail
    and centre of pressure velocities during stair ascent and descent." *Gait &
    Posture* (2011). PubMed 21816614.

18. [RailLoads2022] "Use of handrails for balance and stability: characterizing
    loading profiles in younger adults." *Applied Ergonomics* (2018).
    https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apergo.2018... — peak handrail forces after balance
    loss; height/position dependence. (See also J-Stage AEM 10 (2022) 10_25-0006
    handrail lateral-load measurements.)
